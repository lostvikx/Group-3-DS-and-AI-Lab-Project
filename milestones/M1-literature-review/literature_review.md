# Literature Review: Deepfake Voice and Conversational Scam Detection Using Deep Learning

## Cross-Lingual Degradation and Modality Conflation Limit Existing Voice Scam Defenses

The proliferation of zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS), neural voice conversion (VC), and flow-matching neural vocoders has substantially lowered the barrier for executing voice-cloning fraud over cellular networks and instant messaging platforms ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.mdpi.com%2F1424-8220%2F25%2F7%2F1989); [Li et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2404.13914)). Controlled human perception studies demonstrate that human listeners identify synthetic speech with an average accuracy of only **73.00%**, dropping toward chance levels under noisy or compressed mobile conditions ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.mdpi.com%2F1424-8220%2F25%2F7%2F1989)). This vulnerability is particularly acute in multilingual regions such as India, where callers frequently code-switch across Indo-Aryan and Dravidian languages (such as English, Hindi, Tamil, and Telugu) over narrowband telephony trunks and compressed voice notes ([Ranjan et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fopenreview.net%2Fforum%3Fid%3Ds8pPYRVVTU); [Ahuja et al., 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Frishia.in%2Fpub%2Fahuja2026icfd31k)).

Empirical evaluations across recent literature reveal three structural failure modes in current solutions:

1. **Cross-Lingual and Unseen-Generator Collapse**: Acoustic anti-spoofing models trained on monolingual English studio benchmarks (such as ASVspoof 2019) experience severe domain shift on Indian and South-East Asian languages, with Equal Error Rates (EER) degrading from **0.83%–1.88%** in-domain to **20.25%–68.60%** on unseen Hindi and Tamil synthesis ([Huang et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Faclanthology.org%2F2025.acl-long.493%2F); [Wu et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2509.19865)).
2. **Telephony and Codec Artifact Masking**: Public Switched Telephone Network (PSTN), Voice over IP (VoIP), and messaging codecs (such as 8 kHz A-law, μ-law, G.722, AMR-WB, and Opus) attenuate high-frequency spectral bands above 3.4–4.0 kHz where neural vocoder artifacts typically reside, causing unaugmented self-supervised detectors to fail out-of-domain ([Tak et al., 2022b](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fhtml%2F2111.04433v2); [Maltby et al., 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Frepository.uwl.ac.uk%2Fid%2Feprint%2F15273%2F)).
3. **Orthogonality of Synthetic Voice and Fraudulent Intent**: Acoustic deepfake detectors evaluate only *voice authenticity*, generating false alarms on legitimate automated speech (such as banking IVR systems, transit announcements, and accessibility TTS) while failing to flag human-operated vishing calls. Conversely, text-based conversational fraud detectors ([Ahuja et al., 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Frishia.in%2Fpub%2Fahuja2026icfd31k)) evaluate only *semantic intent*, remaining blind to whether an impersonated family member or official is an AI voice clone.

To address these gaps, the proposed system implements an end-to-end multimodal architecture targeting English, Hindi, Tamil, and Telugu. The pipeline integrates a fine-tuned cross-lingual self-supervised acoustic backbone ([XLS-R-300M](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2111.09296)), telephony channel augmentation ([RawBoost](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fhtml%2F2111.04433v2) and narrowband codec simulation), **3–4 second sliding-window segmentation with 50% overlap** for temporal artifact localization, CTranslate2-accelerated multilingual transcription ([faster-whisper](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FSYSTRAN%2Ffaster-whisper)), semantic scam classification (`TF-IDF + SVM`, fine-tuned [BERT](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Faclanthology.org%2FN19-1423%2F)/[XLM-R](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Faclanthology.org%2F2020.acl-main.747%2F), and zero-shot LLM baselines), and decoupled multimodal decision fusion deployed via a web application and Telegram bot.

### Progression of Acoustic Frontends and Backends Across Challenge Standards

Across the benchmarks synthesized by [Yi et al. (2023)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2308.14970) and [Zhang et al. (2025)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.mdpi.com%2F1424-8220%2F25%2F7%2F1989), acoustic spoofing detection has evolved through three architectural generations:

1. **Handcrafted Spectral Frontends with Statistical or CNN Backends**: Early systems extracted Constant-Q Cepstral Coefficients (`CQCC`), Linear Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (`LFCC`), or Log-Mel Spectrograms paired with Gaussian Mixture Models (`GMM`), Light CNNs (`LCNN`), or `ResNet18` ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.mdpi.com%2F1424-8220%2F25%2F7%2F1989)). While achieving **8.09%–9.57% EER** on clean ASVspoof 2019 Logical Access (LA), fixed filterbanks discard phase information and degrade under telephony compression (**15.62%–25.56% EER** on ASVspoof 2021 LA/DF) and partial spoofing (**45.80%–48.10% EER** on ADD 2022 PF).
2. **End-to-End Raw Waveform and Graph Attention Networks**: Models such as `RawNet2` ([Tak et al., 2022b](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fhtml%2F2111.04433v2)) and `AASIST` ([Jung et al., 2022](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2Fclovaai%2Faasist)) apply learnable `SincNet` filterbanks directly to raw waveforms followed by spectro-temporal graph attention. `AASIST` lowered clean ASVspoof 2019 LA EER to **0.83%** (min t-DCF **0.0275**), but when trained solely on clean English speech, its EER rises to **29.12%** on crowdsourced ASVspoof 5 ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.mdpi.com%2F1424-8220%2F25%2F7%2F1989)) and **43.32%** on multilingual South-East Asian speech ([Wu et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fhtml%2F2509.19865v2)).
3. **Self-Supervised Cross-Lingual Speech Frontends (`XLS-R`) with Data Augmentation**: Pretrained on **436,000 hours** of bona fide speech across **128 languages** ([Babu et al., 2022](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2111.09296)), `wav2vec 2.0 XLS-R-300M` captures contextual vocal-tract dynamics and prosodic transitions. When fine-tuned with `RawBoost` telephony augmentation, `XLS-R-300M` achieves **0.82%–1.00% EER** on ASVspoof 2021 LA and **2.85% EER** on ASVspoof 2021 DF—representing a nearly **90% relative error reduction** over sinc-layer baselines ([Tak et al., 2022a](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2202.12233)).

### Quantitative Analysis of Cross-Lingual and Cross-Generator Transfer

Experimental tables reported in [Huang et al. (2025)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fhtml%2F2507.21463v1) and [Wu et al. (2025)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fhtml%2F2509.19865v2) provide direct empirical support for pairing a cross-lingual self-supervised frontend (`XLS-R-300M`) with regional Indic fine-tuning (`IndicSynth`, `SEA-Spoof`, `SpeechFake`, and `LRLspoof`):

- **Cross-Lingual Advantage of `XLS-R` on Unseen Hindi**: In [SpeechFake (Huang et al., 2025)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fhtml%2F2507.21463v1), when models are trained exclusively on English and Chinese for 50 epochs and tested zero-shot on 44 unseen languages, standalone `AASIST` achieves **0.60% EER** on seen English but degrades to **20.25% EER** on unseen Hindi (`hi`) and **8.49% EER** across the 9 primary languages. Replacing the frontend with `Wav2Vec 2.0 XLS-R` (`W2V+AASIST`) reduces unseen Hindi EER by **95.16% relative**—from **20.25% down to 0.98%**—and achieves **0.23% EER** across 37 additional unseen languages.
- **Commercial vs. Open-Source Spoofing Difficulty in Hindi and Tamil**: In [SEA-Spoof (Wu et al., 2025)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fhtml%2F2509.19865v2), unadapted `MoLEx` exhibits a **35.80% EER** on open-source generators (`VITS-MMS`, `Indic-TTS`, `XTTS-v2`, `F5-TTS`) but degrades to **61.40% EER** on commercial APIs (`ChatGPT-4o-mini-TTS` **67.30% EER**, `ElevenLabs` **65.50% EER**, `MiniMax` **56.50% EER**, `HeyGen` **44.39% EER**). Across languages, commercial Tamil (**68.60% EER**) and Hindi (**64.50% EER**) are the hardest to detect zero-shot, whereas fine-tuning on regional data reduces EER to **0.08%** (Tamil) and **0.06%** (Hindi).
- **Cross-Generator Transfer Across TTS, VC, and Neural Vocoders**: [Huang et al. (2025)](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fhtml%2F2507.21463v1) show that `W2V (XLS-R) + AASIST` trained on diverse TTS data achieves **1.01% EER** on seen TTS, **0.20% EER** on unseen commercial TTS APIs (`BD-UT`), **9.78% EER** on unseen Voice Conversion (`BD-VC`), and **14.34% EER** on unseen Neural Vocoders (`BD-NV`), outperforming standalone `AASIST` across every cross-generator transfer split (**16.85% EER** on `BD-VC` and **25.66% EER** on `BD-NV`).

### Standard Evaluation Metrics Across Subsystems

In accordance with ASVspoof 2019–2024 ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.mdpi.com%2F1424-8220%2F25%2F7%2F1989)), ADD 2023 ([Yi et al., 2023](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2308.14970)), LRLspoof ([Borodin et al., 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2603.02364)), and ICFD-31k ([Ahuja et al., 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Frishia.in%2Fpub%2Fahuja2026icfd31k)), relying solely on binary accuracy is insufficient due to class imbalance and asymmetric misclassification costs. Table 8 specifies the mathematical formulations and operational targets for each evaluation metric across the pipeline.

| Subsystem | Evaluation Metric | Mathematical Formulation / Definition | Direction & Literature Reference Target | Operational Purpose in Proposed System |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acoustic Anti-Spoofing** | **Equal Error Rate (EER %)** | $\text{EER} = \text{FAR}(\theta^*) = \text{FRR}(\theta^*)$, where $\text{FAR}(\theta) = \frac{\text{FP}(\theta)}{N_{\text{spoof}}}$ and $\text{FRR}(\theta) = \frac{\text{FN}(\theta)}{N_{\text{bona}}}$ | Lower is better ($\downarrow$); **<1.0%** in-domain, **<3.0%** telephony/compressed ([Tak et al., 2022a](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2202.12233)) | Primary threshold-independent metric for binary real vs. synthetic voice separation |
| **Acoustic Anti-Spoofing** | **Minimum Normalized Tandem DCF ($\min\text{t-DCF}$)** | $\text{t-DCF}(\theta) = C_1 P_{\text{miss}}^{\text{cm}}(\theta) + C_2 P_{\text{fa}}^{\text{cm}}(\theta)$ normalized by default ASV cost priors ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.mdpi.com%2F1424-8220%2F25%2F7%2F1989)) | Lower is better ($\downarrow$); **0.2066–0.2175** on ASVspoof 2021 LA ([Tak et al., 2022a](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2202.12233)) | Evaluates spoofing countermeasure reliability under realistic biometric/security cost priors |
| **Cross-Lingual Out-of-Domain** | **Spoof Rejection Rate (SRR %) & $1 - \text{SRR}$** | $\text{SRR}(\theta_{\text{cal}}) = \frac{1}{N_{\text{spoof}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{spoof}}} \mathbb{I}(s_i < \theta_{\text{cal}})$ using threshold $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ calibrated on external pool ([Borodin et al., 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Farxiv.org%2Fabs%2F2603.02364)) | Higher SRR ($\uparrow$) / Lower $1-\text{SRR}$ ($\downarrow$) | Evaluates zero-shot rejection on spoof-only low-resource corpora (`LRLspoof`, `IndicSynth`) |
| **Segment Localization** | **Window-Level F1 & Temporal IoU** | $\text{IoU} = \frac{|T_{\text{pred}} \cap T_{\text{gt}}|}{|T_{\text{pred}} \cup T_{\text{gt}}|}$ across 3–4 s sliding windows ($w_k$ with 50% stride overlap) | Higher is better ($\uparrow$); **>0.80** segment overlap on spliced/partial fakes | Validates accuracy of highlighted audio segments shown to users in the Web App and Telegram Bot |
| **ASR Frontend (`faster-whisper`)** | **Word Error Rate (WER %) & Real-Time Factor (RTF)** | $\text{WER} = \frac{S + D + I}{N_{\text{words}}}$; $\text{RTF} = \frac{t_{\text{inference}}}{t_{\text{audio\_duration}}}$ | Lower is better ($\downarrow$); RTF **<0.15** (~4x faster than vanilla Whisper per [SYSTRAN, 2023](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FSYSTRAN%2Ffaster-whisper)) | Ensures low-latency multilingual transcription without bottlenecking bot turnaround time |
| **Scam & Multimodal Fusion** | **Precision, Recall, Macro-F1, & AUROC** | $F_1 = \frac{2 \cdot \text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$; Recall prioritized to minimize missed scams ([Ghotekar et al., 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.ijert.org%2Fdeepfake-audio-detection-for-indian-language); [Ahuja et al., 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Frishia.in%2Fpub%2Fahuja2026icfd31k)) | Higher is better ($\uparrow$); **>92.97% F1** on held-out fraud types ([Ahuja et al., 2026](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Frishia.in%2Fpub%2Fahuja2026icfd31k)) | Evaluates end-to-end scam verdict accuracy across the 4-class fusion taxonomy |

### Multimodal Decision Fusion Matrix

Because synthetic voice generation and fraudulent intent are orthogonal dimensions, the proposed fusion module combines the window-aggregated acoustic deepfake probability $P_{\text{spoof}} = \max_{k} p_{\text{spoof}}(w_k)$ (alongside mean window probability $\bar{p}_{\text{spoof}}$) with the semantic scam probability $P_{\text{scam}}$ from the `faster-whisper` + `XLM-R`/LLM branch. Table 9 defines the four operational quadrants produced by the fusion layer.

| Fusion Quadrant | Acoustic Score ($P_{\text{spoof}}$) | Semantic Score ($P_{\text{scam}}$) | Real-World Scenario in Indian Telecom Context | Unimodal Acoustic Detector Verdict | Unimodal Text Detector Verdict | Proposed Multimodal System Verdict & User Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. AI-Cloned Voice Scam (Critical Threat)** | High ($\ge \tau_{\text{spoof}}$) | High ($\ge \tau_{\text{scam}}$) | Scammer clones a relative's or bank official's voice in Hindi/Tamil/Telugu demanding urgent UPI/OTP transfer | Flags Fake Audio (No scam context) | Flags Scam (Misses voice cloning) | **Critical Alert: AI Deepfake Scam**. Displays overall probability, highlights top 3–4 s spoofed audio windows, and highlights coercive transcript phrases |
| **2. Human Vishing Scam (High Threat)** | Low ($< \tau_{\text{spoof}}$) | High ($\ge \tau_{\text{scam}}$) | Live human scammer impersonating police/customs ("digital arrest" or KYC fraud) using natural voice | **False Negative** (Labels "Real/Safe") | Flags Scam | **High Alert: Human Voice Scam**. Reports authentic human voice ($P_{\text{spoof}}$ low) but flags high semantic fraud risk ($P_{\text{scam}}$ high) with scam category rationale |
| **3. Legitimate Automated Speech (Benign Synthetic)** | High ($\ge \tau_{\text{spoof}}$) | Low ($< \tau_{\text{scam}}$) | Legitimate automated bank IVR balance notification, railway announcement, or TTS accessibility message | **False Positive** (Triggers false alarm) | Labels Benign | **Informational Notice: Synthetic Non-Scam Audio**. Identifies AI-generated TTS voice ($P_{\text{spoof}}$ high) while confirming benign informational content ($P_{\text{scam}}$ low) |
| **4. Authentic Benign Speech (Safe)** | Low ($< \tau_{\text{spoof}}$) | Low ($< \tau_{\text{scam}}$) | Everyday personal or professional voice message in English, Hindi, Tamil, or Telugu | Labels Real | Labels Benign | **Verified Safe: Authentic & Benign**. Confirms natural human speech and non-fraudulent conversational content |
